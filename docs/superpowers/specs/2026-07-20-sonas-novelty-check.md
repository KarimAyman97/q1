## 1. Occupancy rule (from the task brief, applied verbatim)

A paper counts as an **occupant** only if it combines:
(a) architecture search,
(b) small-object-specific objectives or search space, **and**
(c) aerial/small-object benchmarks.

Generic NAS-for-detection (NAS-FPN, DetNAS, MobileDets, 2019–2021) and generic hardware-aware NAS are known from the prior sweep to fail (b) and (c) and were not re-verified here per the task context.

## 4. Required differentiators (PARTIAL verdict — must appear in related work)

Because PhaseNAS-UAV (MDPI *Drones*, 2025) is the closest neighbor, SONAS's related-work section must state explicitly, not just implicitly through results:

1. **Multi-objective Pareto search vs. single-score search.** SONAS runs NSGA-II over an explicit two-objective front {maximize AP_small, minimize GFLOPs} with a hard mAP-preservation constraint against a same-budget baseline; PhaseNAS-UAV screens candidates with a single zero-shot detection score under phase-adaptive LLM guidance — no Pareto front is reported.
2. **Search space is small-object-aware by construction, not emergent.** Every SONAS gene (P2/P5 head on/off, per-level channel budget, fusion topology, block choice per stage, depth/width scale, NMS-free vs. NMS head) encodes a stated small-object hypothesis (design doc §4.1). PhaseNAS-UAV's search space, as documented, is a generic LLM-generated YOLO-variant code space with no declared small-object-specific dimension.
3. **Multi-dataset transfer + design-principle extraction.** SONAS full-trains Pareto-knee architectures and transfer-retrains on VisDrone, UAVDT, and TinyPerson, then extracts and hand-validates 2–3 recurring design principles (design doc §5). PhaseNAS-UAV reports single-run accuracy/efficiency numbers on VisDrone2019 and COCO with no cross-dataset transfer study and no principle-extraction chapter.
4. **Size-stratified reporting.** SONAS reports density-binned AP alongside AP_small as a named objective. No evidence PhaseNAS-UAV reports size-stratified (AP_small/medium/large) breakdowns — its stated results are overall mAP/efficiency.

If, when full text becomes available, PhaseNAS-UAV turns out to already report an explicit AP_small-style objective or a small-object-aware search-space dimension, differentiators 2 and 4 weaken and the verdict should be revisited toward the low end of PARTIAL (heavier citation burden) — this is exactly the kind of update the month-6 re-check (design doc §3.2) exists to catch.

## 5. Hard-to-classify notes

- **PhaseNAS-UAV full text (MDPI *Drones* 9(11):803) was inaccessible** — three fetch attempts (MDPI HTML, MDPI PDF, ResearchGate mirror) all returned HTTP 403 (bot-blocked). The classification above rests on abstract-level search-result snippets only. This was the single largest source of uncertainty in this verdict. **RESOLVED same day — full PDF obtained and read; see §7.** The confirmed absence of small-object objectives, P2 heads, and Pareto search upgrades this row to a clean non-occupant.
- **NASSFG (medical imaging)** is the second hard case: it is the only hit that combines architecture search, multi-objective optimization, and an explicitly small-object-aware search space/objective — but in a different imaging domain (CT/X-ray lesion detection) with no aerial or UAV component. Whether "small-object benchmark" in the brief's occupancy rule was meant to include non-aerial small-object domains is genuinely ambiguous from the rule text alone ("aerial/small-object benchmarks" reads as a slash-joined pair, not strictly conjunctive). Resolved here as: does not occupy SONAS's aerial/UAV niche (different sensor modality, different dataset ecosystem, no transfer relevance), but does establish that "NAS + small-object-aware search space + multi-objective" is not itself a novel combination in the abstract — SONAS's novelty rests on the aerial/UAV specialization and the transfer + principle-extraction study, not on the search paradigm alone. This matches, and slightly sharpens, the design doc's existing framing (§1: "designed by optimization, not by hand," with the three lifts named in §3.3/§8 as search + principle extraction + multi-dataset transfer).
- **YOLO-NASCaps** venue/year could not be pinned down precisely (ResearchGate preprint, apparently an NYU Abu Dhabi internship report building on NASCaps, ICCAD 2020); treated as non-occupant regardless since it fails criterion (c) unambiguously (classification datasets only, no detection benchmark).

## 6. Verdict justification

- No hit satisfies all three occupancy criteria (a)+(b)+(c) with confirmed evidence. The one candidate that could satisfy all three (PhaseNAS-UAV) has unconfirmed criterion (b) and a confirmed absence of multi-objective Pareto search — under the brief's rule as literally applied, it does not occupy.
- The verdict is **PARTIAL, not OPEN**, for two reasons: (1) the full-text access gap on PhaseNAS-UAV means "no small-object objective" is an inference, not a verified fact, and (2) NASSFG proves the general search+small-object-objective+multi-objective combination already exists in a neighboring domain, which means SONAS cannot claim that combination as novel in the abstract — only its aerial/UAV instantiation plus the transfer and principle-extraction study.
- **PARTIAL, not OCCUPIED**: nothing found combines architecture search with a small-object-aware search space/objective *and* an aerial/UAV benchmark *and* multi-objective search *and* multi-dataset transfer *and* principle extraction. The nearest neighbor (PhaseNAS-UAV) matches on dataset and search-existence only.
- Proceed with implementation. Carry the differentiators (§4, confirmed in §7) into the related-work section and treat NASSFG as evidence narrowing (not eliminating) the novelty claim per §5. Re-run this check at month 6 as already planned (design doc §3.2).

## 7. Addendum (2026-07-20, same day): full text of PhaseNAS-UAV obtained and read

Karim supplied the published PDF (*Drones* 2025, 9, 803, 18 pp., accepted 11 Nov 2025). Confirmed facts, replacing the abstract-level inferences above:

- **Search space (their §2.2, Table 2):** anchored on **YOLOv8n/s** with block-level reconfiguration; the extended "YOLOv8*" variant only adds SCDown and PSA toggles under identical n/s budgets. Heads are explicitly **fixed at P3–P5** ("Official n/s backbones; standard neck/head; multi-scale (P3–P5)"). **No P2/high-res head dimension, no head-level allocation, no channel-budget profile, no fusion-topology dimension, no NMS-free option.** Criterion (b) is now a **confirmed absence**, not an inference.
- **Objective (their §2.5.2, Eq. 5):** a single training-free zero-shot score `NAS_det = log(Δ+ε) + B` (multi-scale perturbation response + BatchNorm variance), used as a scalar screen under params/FLOPs budget constraints. **No AP_small term, no Pareto front, single-objective with constraints.** Results (their Table 4) report overall mAP@50:95 only — **no size-stratified AP anywhere in the paper.**
- **Proxy validation (their Appendix B):** the score's mAP correlation is demonstrated only across the homogeneous YOLOv8 {n,s,m,l,x} scale family (5 points, Spearman 1.0) — i.e., family scaling, not ranking of heterogeneous same-budget architectures. SONAS's KT3 proxy-trust protocol (Kendall τ across 10 heterogeneous same-budget genomes, 15 vs 120 epochs) is a directly stronger fidelity test — this becomes **differentiator 5**.
- **No transfer study, no principle extraction** (confirmed by full read; their own Limitations §4 concede a "template boundary" biasing search toward existing components).
- **Citable comparison numbers for SONAS's tables (their Table 4, VisDrone2019, 640, 200 epochs, 3 seeds):** YOLOv8n 18.5 → 18.8 (v8n+) → **19.1 (v8n\*)** mAP@50:95; YOLOv8s 22.5 → 22.7 → **23.4 (v8s\*)**, all at slightly reduced params/FLOPs. These are the numbers a searched SONAS model must beat to claim superiority over the only published NAS-on-VisDrone method — a *stronger* baseline story than hand-designed detectors alone.
- **Net effect on verdict:** differentiators 2 and 4 (§4) hold with full-text evidence; differentiator 5 added (heterogeneous proxy validation). PhaseNAS-UAV is a clean non-occupant and the ideal "closest related work" citation. Verdict effectively **OPEN with one adjacent neighbor**; the month-6 re-check remains scheduled.
